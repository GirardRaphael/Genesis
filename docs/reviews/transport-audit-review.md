# Transport-company readiness audit — 2026-10-10

**Verdict:** useful local decision-support prototype, **not ready for a paying transport company or unattended dispatch**. This audit covers the reviewed local baseline plus the broker extension under integration. It is a release checklist, not evidence that the gates are met. Reviewer: independent Codex verifier task; owned file is this review only. Dependencies: README, PLAN, REQUIREMENTS, DATA-CONTRACT, LOGISTICS-SPEC, DISPATCH, MODEL-POLICY, broker source/tests and official provider/privacy materials. No customer data, private key or live model call was used.

The strengths are real: source-bound road matrices, a bounded OR-Tools candidate search, separate cost and model spend, independent recomputation of pickup/drop-off precedence/capacity/deadlines, durable local run records, and honest synthetic/GPS/traffic labels. They establish an inspectable prototype. They do not establish measured route quality, safe driver operations or commercial reliability. The broker-specific correctness blockers and reproductions are in [broker-review.md](broker-review.md).

## Fix before any external customer or real order

| Priority | Present evidence and risk | Required upgrade and acceptance check |
|---|---|---|
| P0 — identity and tenant isolation | `api.py` deliberately accepts loopback/test clients and has no dispatcher, driver or tenant identity. Trips, plans, run traces and addresses are shared in one local store. Publishing the same API would expose and permit changing another company's operations. | Add authenticated roles and tenant IDs to every write, query, artifact and export; deny cross-tenant IDs even when guessed. Test two tenants across all endpoints, SSE, artifacts, cancellation and background jobs; audit each mutation. Do not expose the current loopback profile on a public bind. |
| P0 — physical truth, cargo and dispatch control | The displayed vehicle position is accelerated simulation, with no driver app, GPS freshness, acceptance, proof of pickup/delivery or road incident acknowledgement. A ready trip is not an accepted job. A local reproduction initially showed a cancelled trip with picked-up but undelivered parcels freeing its van; the current tree now holds that van and reports unresolved cargo. There is no return, unload or transfer workflow to resolve that hold. | Add authenticated driver assignment/accept/reject, timestamped device positions with stale/offline states, scan or signed proof events, audited onboard-cargo recovery, reconciliation and explicit human override. Test loss of connectivity, duplicate events, wrong vehicle, failed delivery, cancellation with onboard freight and clock skew. Keep reassignment blocked until undelivered cargo is resolved. Keep simulated tracks visibly separate. |
| P0 — exact executable route feasibility and cancellation | Broker matrix certification and dispatch materialization were decoupled during integration; reproduced ready trips missed deadlines and omitted service dwell while their matrix certificate remained valid. A later race reproduction left a `ready` trip owned by a `cancelled` plan. | Close all findings in broker-review; validate the committed route and timetable, including service completion, capacity and fleet reservation. Concurrent planning/start/cancel and crash-after-commit tests must show no cancelled plan with startable work, double-booking, phantom completion or duplicate trip. Keep matrix cost estimates distinct from measured route cost. |
| P0 — provider ownership and legal operating terms | Current default Photon and OSRM endpoints are public demos, and the basemap is a free as-is service. [Photon's README](https://github.com/komoot/photon/blob/master/README.md) warns of throttling/bans and no availability guarantee; [OSRM's demo policy](https://github.com/Project-OSRM/osrm-backend/wiki/Api-usage-policy) disallows selling access to its demo and gives no quality guarantee; [OpenFreeMap's terms](https://openfreemap.org/tos/) provide the service as-is. | Use owned or contracted geocoding/routing/tiles with a checked license, capacity budget, monitored data refresh, outage fallback and service expectations. Test at the real fleet's peak request rate, provider downtime and stale road data before taking payment. |
| P0 — customer information governance | Selected addresses and trip history persist locally; no tenant retention/deletion/access process is implemented. Forward searches are sent to a third-party geocoder. Montréal business handling needs a privacy design, not just hiding secrets from model prompts. Québec's [CAI business guidance](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/information-entreprises-privees) covers collection through destruction; Canada's [OPC principles](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/p_principle/) include limiting collection/retention and safeguards where applicable. | Map each data flow and purpose; obtain appropriate privacy/legal review for actual operation; define retention/deletion, access logs, export/erasure, backups and provider disclosure. Test expiry and deletion across DB, traces, backups and logs with synthetic identities before accepting real addresses. |

## Fix before claiming useful operational optimization

| Priority | Present evidence and risk | Required upgrade and acceptance check |
|---|---|---|
| P1 — business constraints | Broker handles at most 10 pickup/drop pairs, four identical vans, one parcel dimension, simple completion deadlines and a fixed 8-hour horizon. It does not model driver shifts/breaks, customer service windows, vehicle restrictions, load dimensions/temperature, depot reloads, failed pickup, returns or required depot return. Operating cost excludes many real components. | Collect actual fleet/order constraints with dispatchers; version the policy per plan; add only validated constraints and independent tests. Reject or explicitly diagnose an order when a required constraint cannot be modeled. Compare same-coverage plans against the company's existing method. |
| P1 — travel/traffic accuracy | OSRM's static driving profile and municipal worksite permits are not measured speed, closures or traffic-aware ETAs. A worksite marker is only contextual. | Obtain a permitted current incident/speed source; measure its coverage and latency; define rerouting policy and recalibrate ETA on held-out trips. Test known closures, stale feeds and a route whose static ETA violates a deadline. Until then label all ETAs static/simulated. |
| P1 — resilience and scale | Verified profile is local SQLite/single worker; PostgreSQL, exporter delivery, backup restoration and 100,000-run scale are unverified. Public providers can disappear, and model explanations can consume budget without making a route possible unless preflight is enforced. | Exercise production DB transactions, lease recovery, idempotent materialization, backups/restores, monitoring and load under expected dispatch concurrency. Alert on held billing reservations, failed jobs, stale map data, late routes and missing GPS. |
| P1 — quantified value | The 20-run Anthropic benchmark uses synthetic S01–S05 scenarios, not company orders. Bounded solver candidates are feasible checks, not proven global optima. User-editable CAD rates are assumptions, and the C$100 cap controls experimental model spend, not tenant invoices. | Pilot with consented historical orders and a documented baseline; report on-time and served coverage, actual driver hours/km, operating cost components, missed orders and uncertainty. Use paired comparable days or matched dispatch periods; have operators adjudicate unacceptable routes. Do not sell percentage savings from synthetic tests. |
| P2 — explanation role | Five models can explain tool evidence; deterministic tools choose assignments and certificates. That is safer than letting a model waive constraints, but it is not autonomous LLM route selection. | Describe the roles accurately; if model proposals are later allowed, constrain their schema, require independent recomputation, record rejected suggestions and evaluate whether their cost improves decisions over the deterministic solver. |

## Ordered go/no-go checklist

1. **Local prototype:** close broker-review correctness findings; rerun domain/runtime/UI/browser checks and review the exact tree. Continue labeling simulations and estimates.
2. **Staffed pilot with synthetic or approved historical orders:** verify provider rights, constraint coverage and route quality against real dispatcher decisions. Keep every action under a human operator.
3. **Operational pilot:** complete tenant/auth/privacy, owned providers, actual driver events, recovery and support processes; rehearse failures and measure service levels.
4. **Commercial launch:** publish only after the P0 gates pass and the P1 metrics show safe, useful value on representative operations. This is an evidence threshold, not a date commitment.

For this audit, no external provider has been contracted, no real traffic/GPS data or customer route corpus was evaluated, and no production security/privacy certification was performed.
